## The OSS funding problem

GitHub Sponsors is free and useful, but limited: just recurring sponsorship. Many modern OSS economic patterns are not covered.

Polar.sh extends the options for maintainers:

- **Recurring sponsors**: monthly or yearly subscription.
- **Premium tiers**: Discord badges, private access, priority support.
- **Issue bounties**: a user wants a bug fixed? They put up a bounty, a dev claims it.
- **Subscriptions**: monetize premium features, advanced docs.
- **One-time purchases**: for large one-off sponsors.

## How it stands out

GitHub Sponsors = just recurring donations. Polar.sh = a structured OSS monetization platform.

Typical workflow:

1. You create your repo on Polar.sh, linked to your GitHub.
2. Configure tiers (free, $5/month, $50/month, etc.).
3. Configure "deals" on certain issues (bounty).
4. Sponsors pay via Polar, you receive the funds (Stripe Connect).

## Bonus: monetizing milestones

You can sell "early access" to a feature in development. Sponsors who pay a tier get early access. This turns OSS development into a light crowdfunding model.

## Vs alternatives

- **GitHub Sponsors**: free, but limited in features.
- **Open Collective**: strong financial transparency, ideal for collectives (multiple maintainers).
- **Patreon**: generalist, less suited to specific OSS.
- **Buy Me a Coffee**: very simple, no structured tiers.

Polar.sh is the most aligned with modern OSS dev workflows: native GitHub integration, per-issue payments, etc.
